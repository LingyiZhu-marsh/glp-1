The Science

GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

Turning a Hormone into a Drug

Two differences remedy GLP-1’s short half-life. DPP-4 inhibitors block the enzyme and raise the body’s own GLP-1 two- to threefold. Their effect is modest because they depend on how much GLP-1 the body still makes.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] GLP-1 receptor agonists are molecules that activate the receptor but resist DPP-4. They give strong, sustained stimulation.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] The first agonist came from an unlikely place. Exendin-4, isolated from Gila monster venom, naturally resists DPP-4 due to its structure. It became exenatide, approved in 2005, to treat diabetes.[@zheng-glp1-receptor-2024][@kim-side-effects-2025]

How are they engineered to last longer?

Each drug uses a different strategy to extend their half-lives, which is why dosing ranges from twice a day to once a week.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

Short-acting drugs mostly lower blood sugar after meals by slowing stomach emptying. Long-acting drugs lower both fasting and after-meal blood sugar, and generally cause more weight loss.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

How does drug signaling differ from the natural hormone?

Natural GLP-1 works in short bursts after meals, mostly close to the gut and its nerves.

The drugs keep the receptor stimulated around the clock, reach every tissue that has the receptor and run at more than 10 times normal (physiological) concentrations.[@jalleh-safety-2026]

This is why the drugs do things the natural hormone barely does, like large weight loss. It also explains many of their side effects.

Where do they act in the brain?

Preclinical studies show that limiting a GLP-1 drug to the brain alone still reduces food intake, so appetite suppression is mainly a brain effect.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026] Most of these drugs don’t cross the blood–brain barrier well, so they reach the brain in other ways:

  • Circumventricular organs: brain areas with leaky barriers, including the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius in the brain stem.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • The vagus nerve: GLP-1R on vagal sensory nerves relays signals to the brain stem, which projects to the hypothalamus and reward areas.[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Tanycytes: specialized glial cells that may carry drugs like semaglutide into the hypothalamus.[@jalleh-safety-2026]

Once there, the drugs act through several circuits:

  • The hypothalamus: they activate POMC/CART neurons, which suppress appetite, and inhibit NPY/AgRP neurons, which drive hunger. This strengthens the body’s natural “stop eating” signals.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025][@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • The reward system: they blunt the dopamine response to food, so food becomes less rewarding.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • A surprise in long-term treatment: a 2026 study in mice found that chronic semaglutide activated AgRP neurons rather than silencing them. That is the opposite of the prevailing view. The brain seems to respond to the calorie deficit, and the AgRP neurons then coordinate metabolic changes that sustain fat loss. Mice without AgRP neurons could not keep the weight off.[@dalton-glp1-study-2026] This is mouse data and not yet confirmed in humans.

Nausea, slower stomach emptying and lower food intake appear to run on partly separate pathways. That raises the hope of future drugs that reduce appetite without as much nausea.[@jalleh-safety-2026]

What do they do in the gut?

GLP-1 RAs slow stomach and small-intestine movement. They do this through vagal and central pathways, raised pressure at the pylorus, and the gut’s own nervous system. This adds to fullness and blunts blood-sugar spikes after meals, and it is also the source of most GI side effects.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@kim-side-effects-2025] With long-acting drugs, the slowing of stomach emptying may partly fade, suggesting the body adapts.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]

Why add more receptors?

A drug can only act where its receptor is found. Activating more than one receptor in the same or different tissues can produce effects larger than either alone.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]

  • Tirzepatide adds the GIP receptor, which is found in the brain, fat cells and beta cells. Most of its benefit seems to come from the brain, with extra metabolic effects from fat tissue.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • GIP and nausea: animal studies suggested GIP might reduce nausea. In head-to-head trials, though, GI side effects with tirzepatide were only slightly lower than with semaglutide (40% vs 43%).[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Triple agonists like retatrutide add the glucagon receptor and are producing even larger weight loss in trials.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026][@hcplive-retatrutide-2026]

Will the receptor stop responding over time?

This is the real question that future research has to answer. GLP-1 RAs stimulate receptors that usually are only interacted with after nutrient intake. Receptors under constant stimulation can be pulled into the cell (internalized) or reduced in number (downregulated). β-arrestin and miR-7 are part of this machinery.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025][@uab-glp1-revolution-2026] So far no clear tolerance has been seen. Widespread use is recent, though: semaglutide was approved for weight management in 2021 and tirzepatide in 2023, so long-term studies are still needed.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
