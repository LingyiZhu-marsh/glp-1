At a Glance

This page is for everyone, with no background needed. It answers common questions about GLP-1 medications so the topic feels approachable.

What is GLP-1?

In everyday conversation, “GLP-1” usually means GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs). This is a class of medications that includes Ozempic, Wegovy, and other newer weight-loss drugs. They copy the effect of a natural gut hormone called glucagon-like peptide-1, or GLP-1.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

How does it help with weight loss?

Your gut releases GLP-1 when you eat. GLP-1 medications are shaped to fit the same receptor the hormone uses, and that receptor is found in many places in the body.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] Most of the appetite effect happens in the brain. When the drug reaches areas in the brainstem and hypothalamus, it strengthens the body’s natural “I’m full” signals. That turns down hunger and cravings before a meal and helps you feel full sooner during it.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025][@uab-glp1-revolution-2026] Many people also say foods they used to crave lose their pull, because the drug dulls the reward signal (dopamine) that tasty food normally triggers.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026] In the stomach, the drug slows digestion, so food stays longer and you feel full after smaller portions.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] In the pancreas, it helps release insulin when blood sugar is high, which is why these drugs were first made for diabetes.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Is it only a weight-loss drug?

No. These medications were first made to treat type 2 diabetes, and weight loss turned out to be a major benefit.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] Today, some are also approved to:

  • lower the risk of heart attack and stroke
  • slow kidney disease in people with diabetes
  • treat sleep apnea
  • treat a serious form of fatty liver disease[@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025][@fda-mash-2025]

They can affect so many things because GLP-1 receptors are found in the pancreas, gut, brain, kidneys, lungs, fat tissue and elsewhere.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Why can’t my body’s own GLP-1 do this?

Natural GLP-1 lasts only about two minutes before an enzyme called DPP-4 breaks it down. Only about 10–15% of it reaches the bloodstream intact.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] In people with type 2 diabetes, the body also responds more weakly to GLP-1.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] GLP-1 medications are built to resist DPP-4, so they stay active for hours or days instead of minutes. That is why some are taken daily and others weekly.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020][@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

What medications are there, and how are they different?

They all target the GLP-1 receptor, but they differ in how long they last, how they’re taken, and how much weight loss they cause.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

Two differences are worth knowing:

  • Tirzepatide hits two receptors. It activates GLP-1 receptors and also receptors for a second gut hormone, GIP.[@morello-emerging-uses-2026][@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • Orforglipron isn’t a protein. It’s a small chemical molecule, so it survives digestion without the special food and water rules the semaglutide pill needs.[@fda-priority-voucher-2026]

In general, longer-acting drugs lower blood sugar and weight more than short-acting ones.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

How much weight do people lose?

In clinical trials:

  • weekly semaglutide: about 16% of body weight over 68 weeks, vs about 6% on placebo[@alexander-treatment-effects-2026]
  • tirzepatide: about 15–21%[@alexander-treatment-effects-2026]
  • the newer pills: roughly 12–14%[@healio-wegovy-pill-2025][@fda-priority-voucher-2026]

These come from different trials, so they can’t be compared exactly. People also tend to lose less in everyday life than in trials, though the loss is still meaningful.[@alexander-treatment-effects-2026] A large 2026 review found results were similar across age, race, ethnicity and starting weight. Women lost somewhat more than men on average (about 11% vs 7%). Averages also hide a lot of individual variation, and some trial participants lost little or no weight.[@alexander-treatment-effects-2026]

What happens if you stop?

These drugs only work while they’re in your body. They don’t permanently change your own GLP-1 or its receptors. When you stop, the drug clears out, your appetite signals go back to their usual short bursts after meals, and weight often comes back.[@yale-glp1-wonder-drugs-2024]

What are the side effects?

The most common ones are in the stomach and gut: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation. Nausea is reported most, because the drugs slow how fast food leaves your stomach.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] In one study, 18% of people on semaglutide reported nausea vs 7% on a different diabetes drug, and it faded over time.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] Side effects depend on dose, which is why doses start low and rise slowly.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026] Nausea is also the top reason people stop taking these drugs.[@jalleh-safety-2026]

A few less common concerns are being watched closely: gallstones, food staying in the stomach before surgery, and eye problems in some people with diabetes. Early worries about pancreatitis have largely not held up in large, long-term trials.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026] The Concerns and Side Effects page covers these in detail. Ask a medical professional for advice about your own situation.

Does it cause cancer?

The short answer: there’s no clear sign that it does in most people, but research is ongoing.

  • Pancreas: early reports raised worries about pancreatic cancer, but large trials and reviews have not found an increase.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Thyroid: the concern comes mostly from rodents, whose thyroid cells carry many GLP-1 receptors. Human thyroid cells mostly don’t.[@jalleh-safety-2026] People with a personal or family history of a rare thyroid cancer (medullary thyroid cancer) or a related genetic syndrome (MEN2) are told not to take these drugs.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • The bigger picture: obesity and diabetes themselves raise cancer risk, so it is hard to separate the drug’s effect from the disease’s.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Are cheaper or compounded versions the same?

No. “Compounded” versions are made by pharmacies, not the original manufacturer, and they are not FDA-approved. That means they have not been reviewed for safety, effectiveness or quality.[@fda-unapproved-glp1-2026] The FDA has reported dosing errors, warm-shipped products, fake labels, and versions using different chemical forms of the drug.[@fda-unapproved-glp1-2026][@williams-compounded-glp1-2026] Approved medicines come in pre-measured pens or pills. Many compounded products come in vials, so you measure the dose yourself, and mistakes can result in doses 10 to 20 times the intended amount.[@williams-compounded-glp1-2026]

Is it really a “miracle drug”?

The short answer is no. Long answer: it has been called the “miracle drug of the 21st century” because one medicine affects blood sugar, appetite, the heart, the kidneys, and possibly more.[@yale-glp1-wonder-drugs-2024] They are an important breakthrough with lots of caveats, and besides side effects and some failure in real trials, long-term effects of decades of use are still unknown.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
