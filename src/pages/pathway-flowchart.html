How to read this flowchart

This flowchart follows GLP-1 from a meal to its effects across the body. Read it from top to bottom. Sections 1–3 show how GLP-1, or a GLP-1 drug, reaches its receptor and switches it on. Section 4 shows what happens next in each tissue, Section 5 shows the molecules that fine-tune the signal, and Section 6 compares early safety worries with what human studies have found. Each section below gives a short summary, more detail from my research, and links to where the topic is covered on the rest of this site.

  • Blue boxes: the drug (GLP-1 receptor agonists)
  • Orange boxes: the body’s own GLP-1
  • Purple outline: animal or lab evidence only
  • Orange outline: a side effect or safety concern

1 · Making GLP-1

After a meal, L cells in the gut make GLP-1 and release it in two waves. Together with GIP, it drives the “incretin effect,” the extra insulin released when glucose is eaten rather than injected.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]

In more detail:

  • Made from proglucagon: the enzyme PC1/3 cuts proglucagon into GLP-1 (7–37) and GLP-1 (7–36) amide, the main form in humans. A small group of brain-stem neurons (PPG neurons) also make GLP-1 and use it as a neurotransmitter.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Two waves: the early wave (15–30 minutes) starts before food even reaches the L cells, triggered by nerve signals from the upper gut (acetylcholine and gastrin-releasing peptide). The late wave (90–120 minutes) comes when nutrients reach the L cells directly.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Release control: GABA and glycine increase release, while somatostatin holds it back; blocking somatostatin increased GLP-1 release about eightfold. In obesity, high levels of fatty acids in the blood blunt release after meals.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • The incretin effect: GLP-1 and GIP account for about 50–70% of the insulin released after a meal. Active GLP-1 rises from 5–15 pM while fasting to three to five times higher after eating, then returns to baseline within 2–3 hours.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]
  • The type 2 diabetes side panel: in type 2 diabetes, the insulin response to GLP-1 is blunted, though very high GLP-1 levels can overcome it. The response to GIP fails even at high doses, which is why treatment focused on GLP-1. Inflammation is also higher, including in the gut’s immune network that GLP-1 normally helps calm.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020][@yusta-enteric-immunity-2015]

2 · Two paths to the receptor

The body’s own GLP-1 is broken down within minutes, so it acts in short bursts. GLP-1 drugs are built to resist that breakdown, so they keep receptors switched on for hours to days.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

In more detail:

  • The natural path: the enzyme DPP-4 cuts GLP-1 right after the alanine at position 8, and the kidneys clear the inactive fragments. Only 10–15% of released GLP-1 reaches the blood intact.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] DPP-4 sits on cell surfaces throughout the body and circulates in the blood, so there is no escaping it.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]
  • Built to last: exenatide comes from exendin-4, a peptide from Gila monster venom that naturally resists DPP-4.[@zheng-glp1-receptor-2024] Liraglutide carries a fatty acid that binds the blood protein albumin, so only 1–2% floats free and the rest acts as a reservoir (about 13 hours). Semaglutide binds albumin more strongly (5.7–6.7 days). Dulaglutide is fused to part of an antibody, and oral semaglutide uses an absorption enhancer (SNAC) to get through the stomach.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]
  • Steady activation: because they aren’t broken down, the drugs keep receptors on between meals, in every tissue that has them, at more than 10 times normal levels. This is why they cause effects the natural hormone barely does, including large weight loss and many side effects.[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Newer designs: tirzepatide also activates the GIP receptor, and retatrutide adds the glucagon receptor on top of both.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • If the drug stops: the drugs don’t permanently change the body’s own GLP-1 or its receptors, so once the drug clears, signaling returns to short after-meal bursts.

3 · At the receptor

When GLP-1 or a drug binds its receptor, the receptor switches on a chain of signals: Gs, then cAMP, then PKA and Epac2. Other proteins later turn the signal down.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

In more detail:

  • Where the receptor is: GLP-1R is most common in the pancreas, brain and gut. Heart muscle cells and liver cells have very little, so benefits to those organs are probably indirect.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026][@uab-glp1-revolution-2026][@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025]
  • The two effectors: cAMP activates PKA and Epac2, and Epac2 also works through a partner protein, Rap1. What they do next depends on the tissue, which is why the flowchart branches in Section 4.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Turning down: β-arrestin 1 desensitizes the receptor after use.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025] It also starts its own signals through ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT, which feed into the long-term effects in beta cells.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Long-term use: receptors under constant stimulation can be pulled inside the cell or reduced in number. No clear tolerance has been seen yet, but widespread use is recent.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]
  • The GIP receptor: found in the brain, fat cells and beta cells. Activating both receptors at once can produce effects larger than either alone, and most of tirzepatide’s benefit seems to come from the brain.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026]

4 · Effects in each tissue

The same receptor signal does different things in different tissues. Some effects are direct, through receptors in that tissue, and others are indirect, through weight loss and lower inflammation.

In more detail:

  • Pancreas, beta cells: K-ATP channels close, calcium rises, and SNARE proteins release insulin, but only when blood sugar is high. Longer term, CREB, HIF-1α and mTOR support beta-cell survival and insulin production.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Pancreas, alpha cells: glucagon release falls, lowering blood sugar further.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] Short-acting drugs mainly lower after-meal glucose, while long-acting drugs lower fasting glucose too.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]
  • Brain: most drugs barely cross the blood–brain barrier, so they act through “leaky” brain-stem areas (the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius), the vagus nerve, and possibly tanycytes.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026] In the hypothalamus, appetite-suppressing POMC/CART neurons switch on and hunger-driving NPY/AgRP neurons switch off.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025] The drugs also blunt the dopamine reward from food.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026] A 2026 study in mice found a surprise: with long-term semaglutide, AgRP “hunger” neurons actually switched on rather than off, responding to the calorie deficit and helping sustain fat loss. Mice without these neurons couldn’t keep the weight off. This hasn’t been confirmed in humans yet.
  • Stomach and gut: signals through the vagus nerve and higher pressure at the pylorus slow stomach emptying.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] This slowing may partly fade with long-acting drugs.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020] It also means food can stay in the stomach before procedures (56% of users vs 19% of non-users in one study).[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Gallbladder: GLP-1 lowers cholecystokinin, which slows gallbladder emptying, and rapid weight loss concentrates cholesterol in bile. Both lead to gallstones.[@kim-side-effects-2025]
  • Immune system: GLP-1 receptors on some T cells and brain neurons help calm inflammation body-wide, and markers like CRP, TNF-α and IL-6 fall, partly regardless of weight loss.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026][@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025]
  • Heart and blood vessels: GLP-1 lowers oxidative stress in blood vessels, widens them and improves blood pressure.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] In the SELECT trial, semaglutide cut major cardiovascular events by 20%, and the benefit didn’t depend on how much weight was lost.[@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025]
  • Liver: liver cells lack GLP-1 receptors, so benefits come through weight loss, better insulin sensitivity and less inflammation.[@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025] In interim trial results, MASH (fatty liver with inflammation) resolved in 63% of people on semaglutide vs 34% on placebo, leading to FDA approval for MASH in 2025.[@fda-mash-2025]
  • Kidneys: GLP-1 increases salt and water loss in urine.[@latif-glp1-comparison-2026] In the FLOW trial, semaglutide cut major kidney events by 24% in people with type 2 diabetes and kidney disease.[@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025]
  • Animal studies only: GLP-1 activated brown fat in rats[@kowalska-glp1-2026], and in animal and cell studies it supported bone formation, reduced muscle wasting, and protected neurons in Parkinson’s disease models.[@zheng-glp1-receptor-2024]

5 · Regulating the GLP-1 signal

MicroRNAs are small RNAs that act like dimmer switches for genes. Some turn GLP-1 signaling down, and GLP-1 itself changes others.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025]

In more detail:

  • Turning the signal down: miR-204 lowers the receptor in islets, and deleting it in animals restored the response to GLP-1 drugs. miR-7 targets β-arrestin 1 and weakens GLP-1’s cAMP signal. miR-23b, released from fat tissue in tiny packages called exosomes, lowers receptor production, one way excess fat itself can weaken the GLP-1 system.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025]
  • Changed by GLP-1: GLP-1 lowers miR-34a, protecting beta cells from fat overload. It also lowers miR-19b and miR-758, which raises ABCA1, a transporter that moves cholesterol out of liver cells, and lowers miR-23a, helping liver cells survive high blood sugar.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025]
  • Why it matters: microRNAs travel in blood, urine and saliva, so researchers are studying them as markers of disease and as predictors of who will respond to diabetes drugs.[@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025]

6 · Concerns

Some safety concerns came from the biology itself: if GLP-1 makes some cells grow, could it make them grow too much? Several of these fears came from animal studies and haven’t held up in large human trials.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]

In more detail:

  • Pancreas: rodent studies showed changes in the pancreatic ducts, and the drugs do raise pancreatic enzymes in people. But in the LEADER trial there were 18 cases of pancreatitis on liraglutide vs 23 on placebo, and large trials haven’t shown more pancreatitis or pancreatic cancer.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Thyroid: rodent thyroid C cells are covered in GLP-1 receptors, but human C cells mostly aren’t, so the rodent tumors may not apply to people.[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Eyes: a fast drop in blood sugar can temporarily worsen existing diabetic retinopathy, the same pattern seen with intensive insulin.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Mood: reporting databases showed signals of suicidal thoughts, but a review of 80 randomized trials found no added risk of depression, suicide or psychosis.[@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Lean mass: about 25–40% of the weight lost is lean mass. Human muscle appears to lack GLP-1 receptors, so this is likely a result of weight loss rather than a direct effect.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]
  • Cancer cells in the lab: liraglutide slowed the growth of thyroid cancer cells, and exendin-4 slowed prostate cancer cells, but these are cell studies only.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
