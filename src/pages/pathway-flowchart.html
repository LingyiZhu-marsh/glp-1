Pathway Flowchart

How to read this flowchart

This flowchart follows GLP-1 from a meal to its effects across the body. Read it from top to bottom. Sections 1–3 show how GLP-1, or a GLP-1 drug, reaches its receptor and switches it on. Section 4 shows what happens next in each tissue, Section 5 shows the molecules that fine-tune the signal, and Section 6 compares early safety worries with what human studies have found.

  • Blue boxes: the drug (GLP-1 receptor agonists)
  • Peach boxes: the body’s own GLP-1
  • Purple outline: animal or lab evidence only
  • Orange outline: a side effect or safety concern

1 · Making GLP-1

This section shows how the body makes and releases GLP-1. After a meal, L cells in the gut cut GLP-1 out of proglucagon and release it in two waves: early (15–30 minutes) and late (90–120 minutes). Release is boosted by GABA and glycine and held back by somatostatin and high fatty acids. GLP-1 and GIP released with a meal cause the “incretin effect”: glucose eaten by mouth triggers more insulin than the same glucose given through an IV.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

The side panel, “Why type 2 diabetes needs a stronger signal,” explains that in type 2 diabetes the body’s insulin response to GLP-1 is disrupted and inflammation is higher, so its natural signal isn’t enough.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

2 · Two paths to the receptor

This section compares the body’s own GLP-1 with the drug. Natural GLP-1 is cut by DPP-4 within about 2 minutes, so it only works in short bursts after meals. GLP-1 drugs are built to resist DPP-4, so they last hours to days and keep receptors switched on throughout the body.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

3 · At the receptor

This section shows what happens when GLP-1 or a drug binds its receptor. The receptor switches on Gs, which makes cAMP, which in turn switches on PKA and Epac2. β-arrestin later turns the receptor down, and tirzepatide also activates a second receptor, the GIP receptor.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

4 · Effects in each tissue

This section shows where the signal goes next. The same receptor signal produces different effects in each tissue: insulin release in the pancreas, fullness in the brain, and slower digestion in the gut. It also shows effects on the heart, blood vessels, kidneys and immune system. Boxes with a purple outline are animal findings only.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@latif-glp1-comparison-2026]

5 · Regulating the GLP-1 signal

This section shows microRNAs, small RNAs that act like dimmer switches for genes. Some lower GLP-1 or its receptor, weakening the signal. Others are changed by GLP-1 itself, which helps protect beta cells and liver cells.

6 · Concerns

This section compares early safety worries with what human studies show. Fears about the pancreas and thyroid came mostly from animal studies and haven’t held up in large human trials. Effects on the eyes and muscle still need more research.[@kim-side-effects-2025][@jalleh-safety-2026]
