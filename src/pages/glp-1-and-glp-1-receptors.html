The Science

GLP-1 and GLP-1 Receptors

Structure and Secretion of GLP-1

GLP-1 is a peptide hormone of 30–31 amino acids. An enzyme called prohormone convertase 1/3 (PC1/3) cuts it out of a larger precursor protein, proglucagon. The active forms are GLP-1 (7–37) and its amidated form GLP-1 (7–36); the main form in humans. Most GLP-1 is secreted from L-type enteroendocrine cells in the lower small intestine and colon. A small number of neurons in the brain stem (preproglucagon, or PPG, neurons) also make it and use it as a neurotransmitter.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@al-noshokaty-glp1-2025]

Secretion happens in two phases:

  • Early phase: 15–30 minutes after a meal, likely triggered by nerve and hormone signals from the upper gut before food reaches the L cells. Acetylcholine and gastrin-releasing peptide help relay this “proximal-distal” signal.
  • Late phase: 90–120 minutes after a meal, when nutrients reach the L cells directly.

GABA and glycine stimulate secretion, and somatostatin inhibits it.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

The Incretin Effect

Glucose eaten by mouth triggers a much bigger insulin response than the same glucose given through an IV. The difference comes from gut hormones released with the meal, mainly GLP-1 and GIP, which together account for about 50–70% of insulin released after eating.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]

  • Fasting levels of active GLP-1 are only 5–15 pM. After a meal they rise three- to fivefold, peak around 30–45 minutes, and return to baseline within 2–3 hours.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Insulin release driven by GLP-1 depends on glucose, so it happens mainly when blood sugar is high. That keeps the risk of low blood sugar low.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Natural Signal of GLP-1

GLP-1 has a half-life of about 2 minutes. The enzyme DPP-4 cuts the peptide right after the alanine at position 8, leaving inactive fragments that the kidneys clear. Only 10–15% of the GLP-1 that L cells release reaches the circulation intact.[@kowalska-glp1-2026] DPP-4 sits on cell surfaces throughout the body and also circulates in the blood.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]

GLP-1 Receptor

The GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) is encoded by the GLP1R gene on chromosome 6. It is a class B (class 2) G protein-coupled receptor that crosses the membrane seven times.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026] Its found mainly the pancreatic islets, the brain and the GI tract, with lower levels in the kidney, lung, eye and other tissues.[@kowalska-glp1-2026][@jalleh-safety-2026]  Yet, some organs that benefit from GLP-1 drugs have surprisingly little receptor. Heart muscle cells (cardiomyocytes) and liver cells (hepatocytes) express little GLP-1R, so many heart and liver benefits are probably indirect. They likely come through weight loss, the brain, and receptors on immune cells and blood vessels.[@uab-glp1-revolution-2026][@gonzalez-rellan-benefits-2025] There are also species differences in receptors, GLP-1R is plentiful on rodent thyroid C cells but scarce on human ones. This is one reason animal cancer findings don’t translate directly to humans.[@jalleh-safety-2026]

The Signal of the Receptor

GLP-1R follows the classic peptide-hormone route:

  1. GLP-1 binds, and the receptor activates its stimulatory G protein (Gs).
  2. Gs switches on adenylate cyclase, which turns ATP into cyclic AMP (cAMP).
  3. cAMP activates two effectors, protein kinase A (PKA) and Epac2, which carry out most later steps.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Releasing Insulin in Beta Cells

  1. PKA and Epac2 signaling closes ATP-sensitive potassium channels (made of Kir6.2 pore subunits and SUR1 regulatory subunits).
  2. With potassium no longer leaking out, the membrane depolarizes. This opens L-type voltage-gated calcium channels (CaV1.2 and CaV1.3), and calcium flows in.
  3. That calcium triggers calcium-induced calcium release from internal stores through IP3 receptors and ryanodine receptors, raising cytoplasmic calcium further.
  4. Rising calcium, together with PKA and Epac2, drives insulin granule exocytosis. SNARE complexes (SNAP-25, Snapin, Rim2) dock the granules at the membrane and fuse them.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

The whole process is glucose-dependent, so the extra insulin comes mainly when blood sugar is high.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Long Term Effects in Beta Cells

  • CREB: PKA phosphorylates CREB in the nucleus. CREB switches on genes for beta-cell survival, insulin production and secretory capacity, and helps protect beta cells from death caused by inflammatory cytokines (proposed CREB–CITED2–p21 axis).[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • HIF-1α: works with CREB to express beta-cell genes, including SNARE components and enzymes that make insulin.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • PI3K/AKT -> mTOR and MAPK/ERK1/2: boost protein and insulin synthesis and support cell growth. Some of this signaling involves β-arrestin.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]

Turning off the Signal

These receptors' signals are regulated, they are not activated forever. β-arrestin 1 (BARR1) helps desensitize GLP-1R after it is activated. A microRNA, miR-7, targets BARR1, and GLP-1 and miR-7 regulate each other. Blocking miR-7 restored and boosted cAMP production in beta cells.[@zheng-glp1-receptor-2024]

How do microRNAs control GLP-1 and its receptor?

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small RNAs, about 22 nucleotides long, that bind target mRNAs and silence them. Several control GLP-1 or its receptor:[@zheng-glp1-receptor-2024]

miRNAWhat it does
miR-204Controlled by TXNIP; directly lowers GLP-1R in islets. Deleting it restores receptor levels and drug response.

GLP-1 in the Immune System

In mice, GLP-1R is found on intraepithelial lymphocytes (IELs), immune cells that sit in the gut lining. Exendin-4 (an early GLP-1 drug) raised cAMP in these cells and reduced their cytokine output. Mice without the receptor had altered gut gene expression, different gut bacteria, and more severe intestinal injury in a colitis model.[@yusta-enteric-immunity-2015] This gut immune network may help explain the anti-inflammatory effects of GLP-1 drugs.

Disruption in Type 2 Diabetes

  • The incretin effect is impaired. The insulin response to GLP-1 is blunted, though very high (supraphysiologic) GLP-1 levels can overcome it.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]
  • The GIP response is impaired too. High-dose GIP does not fix it, which is why drug development first focused on GLP-1.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]
  • Obesity interferes with release. High levels of fatty acids in the blood, common in obesity, may interfere with GLP-1 release after meals.[@kowalska-glp1-2026]
  • Inflammation adds to it. Ongoing inflammation suggests the immune controls described above are disturbed.[@yusta-enteric-immunity-2015]

Put together, the body’s response is blunted and the natural signal is too brief. Raising GLP-1 levels and keeping them high can overcome both, and that is the rationale behind GLP-1 receptor agonists.[@gilbert-glp1-dpp4-2020]
